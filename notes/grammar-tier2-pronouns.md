# Tier 2 Q8: the pronoun system

*Study run 2026-08-05. Script: [`../scripts/grammar_tier2_pronouns.py`](../scripts/grammar_tier2_pronouns.py) ·
output: `data/processed/grammar_tier2_pronouns.csv` · agenda:
[`grammar-plan.md`](grammar-plan.md) §4 Q8 · evidence policy: same file §5.*

**Bottom line up front.** **Seven words: three persons × two numbers, plus one invariant
reflexive.** No gender, no clusivity, no dual, no politeness distinction, no case forms, no
special possessives. **Alignment stays dead** — the deferral holds, because the route back to
it was pronoun case and contact languages do not have it.

This study contains **the largest single vote against a population majority in the project**:
dropping the politeness distinction goes against 85% of the world's L1 mass.

---

## 1. The shape: three persons, two numbers, nothing else

| | restricted pidgin | expanded pidgin | creole |
|---|---|---|---|
| **no person syncretism** (all three persons distinct) | **9 of 9** | 8 of 9 | 47 of 54 |
| **no dual form** | **7 of 9** | 7 of 9 | 50 of 54 |
| **no inclusive/exclusive distinction** | **9 of 9** | 7 of 9 | 49 of 54 |

**Clusivity** is a majority *by language count* — 52.6% of Grambank's 2,289 — and a minority
*by people*: **37.3% of L1**, and WALS 39A puts "no inclusive/exclusive" at 66.3% of L1. The
same by-language/by-people reversal as everywhere else. Rule 2 is neutral (LOST 1, GAINED 2,
KEPT 2), so this is decided by the pidgin column and the population count agreeing.

**Duals** are 26.3% of languages but **1.4% of world L1** — one of the sharpest
language/people gaps in the project, and the cheapest rejection in it.

**Decision: 1/2/3 × singular/plural = six forms.**

## 2. No gender — and this one has a real population cost

| | restricted pidgin | expanded pidgin | creole |
|---|---|---|---|
| **no gender distinctions in pronouns** | **9 of 9** | 7 of 9 | 40 of 56 |

**Rule 2 is emphatic: gender in 3rd-person pronouns is LOST 9, KEPT 2, GAINED 1.** It was
available in the input and adults dropped it — the model case the rule was written for.

But the population count runs the other way. WALS 44A puts "no gender distinctions" at 67.2%
of languages and only **22.1% of L1** — so **roughly 78% of the world's L1 mass has gender
somewhere in its pronouns.** Grambank agrees in direction (3rd-person gender: 24.1% of
languages, 40.9% of L1, 50.6% of total speakers).

**Decision: no gender.** The asymmetry that settles it is about *learning direction*: a
speaker of a gendered system learning a genderless one has nothing new to learn and only an
expressive nuance to give up, whereas a speaker of a genderless system learning a gendered
one must acquire an obligatory judgment they have never had to make. The burden is
one-directional, and it is exactly what the 9-to-2 Rule 2 result records adults doing when
they had the choice.

## 3. Politeness: the largest vote against a majority in the project

| | restricted pidgin | expanded pidgin | creole |
|---|---|---|---|
| **no pronominal politeness distinction** | **7 of 9** | 6 of 9 | 29 of 53 |
| binary distinction | 2 | 2 | 13 |
| titles used as 2nd person forms | 0 | 0 | 8 |
| multiple distinctions | 0 | 0 | 3 |

The world, WALS 45A (n=205):

| | % languages | **% of L1** |
|---|---|---|
| no politeness distinction | 65.4 | **15.0** |
| binary (T/V) | 23.9 | **58.4** |
| multiple | 7.3 | 17.3 |
| pronouns avoided for politeness | 3.4 | 9.4 |

**85% of the world's L1 mass has some grammatical politeness distinction in its pronouns.**
Only 15% has none. This is a bigger population gap than the numeral classifiers we rejected
in Q7 (36%), and it is the largest in the project.

**Decision: no politeness distinction.** Three reasons, and the second is the real one:

1. Contact languages agree — 7 of 9 restricted pidgins, 6 of 9 expanded.
2. **It is an obligatory social judgment on every utterance addressed to a person.** Of all
   the obligatory categories a grammar can impose, this is the one with the highest
   frequency *and* the highest cost of error: getting tense wrong is a mistake, getting T/V
   wrong is an insult. A learner who cannot yet read the social register of a situation
   cannot open their mouth. For a language whose entire purpose is use between strangers
   with no shared culture, an obligatory politeness choice is close to a design fault.
3. It remains **expressible lexically** — titles and honorific vocabulary — which is what 8
   creoles in the sample do. Nothing is lost except the obligation.

**This must appear in the final bias audit** ([`grammar-plan.md`](grammar-plan.md) §1)
alongside the classifier decision. Speakers of Hindi, Japanese, Korean, German, French,
Spanish, Russian and Javanese will all find the absence conspicuous.

## 4. No case — so alignment stays dead

This is the question [`principles.md`](principles.md) §7 flagged when alignment was deferred:
*an isolating language can still have I/me — English does — and if pronouns carry case,
alignment has to be decided after all.*

| | restricted pidgin | expanded pidgin | creole |
|---|---|---|---|
| **neutral alignment in pronouns** (APiCS 59) | **5 of 9** | **6 of 9** | 11 of 54 |
| accusative alignment | 4 | 3 | **43 of 54** |
| **no dependent subject/object forms** (APiCS 17) | **9 of 9** | 5 of 10 | 15 of 54 |

**The gradient is the clearest in this study.** Restricted pidgins: 9 of 9 have no distinct
subject/object forms at all, and neutral alignment leads 5:4. Expanded pidgins: neutral leads
6:3. Creoles: accusative wins 43:11. Pronoun case is something **nativisation adds and
inheritance supplies** — English *I/me* and French *je/moi* are right there in the input —
and **Rule 2 confirms it: case on pronominal core arguments is LOST 7, KEPT 3, GAINED 1.**

The world is more evenly split than the pidgins: Grambank GB071 puts morphological case on
pronominal core arguments at 45.2% of languages but **61.1% of L1**.

**Decision: no case forms. Pronouns are invariant.** *I see he*, not *I see him*.

**Consequence: the alignment deferral holds.** With no case on nouns (Tier 0), no verbal
agreement, and now no case on pronouns, there is no morphology anywhere that could carry an
alignment contrast. The one route back that §7 identified is closed, and alignment can stay
deferred indefinitely rather than merely for now.

## 5. What else was considered

**Reflexive: yes, one invariant word.** GB305 puts a phonologically independent reflexive
pronoun at 49.8% of languages but **89.7% of L1** — the highest population figure in this
study — and Rule 2 keeps it (KEPT 7, LOST 3). One invariant word (*self*) serving all persons
and numbers costs one item and no paradigm: *I see self*, *they see self*.

**Special possessive pronouns: no.** GB313 is 36.7% of languages, 40.9% of L1. We do not need
them: Q11 puts the possessor before the noun, so the plain pronoun in that slot *is* the
possessive — *I house* = "my house". Rule 2 is mildly positive (KEPT 7, LOST 2), but this is
a case where our own word order makes the category redundant rather than rejected.

**Logophoric pronouns: no.** 6.7% of languages, **1.3% of L1**, and 0 of 12 contact pairs
have one. The cheapest rejection in the project.

**Third person = demonstrative: considered, rejected.** WALS 43A shows most languages relate
them somehow ("unrelated" is 44.2% of languages but only 29.4% of L1), and merging them would
save a word. Rejected because Q7 already gave the demonstrative **article duty** — making it
also the 3rd-person pronoun would have one form carrying three jobs, and the ambiguity cost
exceeds the one word saved. Recorded because the data mildly favours the merge.

**3rd-person-plural as the nominal plural word: left to the lexicon.** 13 creoles use the
same form for both (Tok Pisin *ol*), plus 9 more with partial overlap — but 6 of 9 restricted
pidgins have no nominal plural word at all, so the pidgin column is silent. Since Q7 made the
plural word optional vocabulary, whether it is *identical to* "they" is a lexicon-stage
economy rather than a grammar decision. Flagged there.

## 7. Honest limits

- **9 restricted pidgins.** As always.
- **The politeness decision overrides 85% of world L1** on the strength of nine pidgins and
  an argument about obligatory social judgment. The argument is good but it is an argument,
  not a measurement, and this is the item in Tier 2 most likely to draw objection.
- **The gender decision overrides ~78% of world L1**, mitigated by the one-directional
  learning-burden argument in §2 — also an argument rather than a measurement.
- **WALS 45A rests on 205 languages and 44A on 375**, both small, and their by-L1 columns are
  dominated by a handful of large languages. The direction is not in doubt; the exact
  percentages are.
- **APiCS 59 codes only two values** (neutral vs accusative), so it cannot show ergative or
  split systems in contact languages. For this decision that does not matter — we are
  choosing "no case" — but the feature should not be quoted as a general alignment survey.
