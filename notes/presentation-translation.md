# The presentation script, in interlang

*Built 2026-08-06 under time pressure, for Patrick to deliver live. **This is not a
product of the lexicon pipeline** — roughly 30 of its words were coined by hand under the
phonotactic rules because the vocabulary generator did not finish in time. Provenance is
marked per word in §2 so nothing here is mistaken for optimizer output later.*

**What is real:** the grammar is entirely as decided (§3.2 of [`principles.md`](principles.md),
20 words and 7 rules), and ~110 of the words come from
[`lexicon-milestone1.md`](lexicon-milestone1.md). **What is provisional:** the hand-coined
vocabulary in §2b, which leans Romance because that was fast and defensible for a technical
register — and which therefore makes this text **more Latinate than the finished lexicon will
be**. Do not cite this as evidence of the lexicon's donor spread.

## 3. Honest limits

- **~30 words are hand-coined**, not selected by the optimizer against the donor pool. They
  are phonotactically legal and collision-checked against the 138-word lexicon, and nothing
  more.
- **The hand-coined set is heavily Romance/Latin**, including *every preposition*. That is the
  opposite of the lexicon's actual design goal (24 families, none above 6%), and it makes this
  text unrepresentative of the finished language in exactly the dimension the talk claims
  credit for. **Say "these are placeholder words" if anyone asks about the vocabulary spread.**
- **The grammar, by contrast, is exactly as decided** and can be defended line by line.
- **Two agents clobbered `scripts/lexicon_milestone1.py`** while this was being written (see
  the concurrency note in the session record); the 138-word CSV survived intact and verified
  (0 duplicate forms), but the script on disk is a merge of two incompatible edits and needs
  reconciling before it is run again.
- Simplifications made for speakability, all recorded so the English and interlang do not
  silently diverge: "regardless of their first language" became *their first language is not
  special*; "kinda sucks" became *very similar to European languages*; the loanword sentence
  was split in two.
